# What cannot be seen

Every case in this corpus is a case where something went wrong badly enough to trigger an investigation. That is the selection rule for the entire evidence base of this subject, and it has a consequence that is easy to state and hard to work around.

**There is no denominator.**

Nobody investigates the flights where a first officer raised a concern and the captain acted on it. No authority publishes a report on the approach that was stabilised because somebody called it. The recordings from those flights are overwritten, on a loop, within hours. The evidence of coordination working is systematically destroyed by design, while the evidence of coordination failing is preserved, transcribed, analysed and published.

This means the accident corpus can establish that poor coordination appears in accidents. It cannot establish how often good coordination prevents them, because the comparison case is unrecorded.

## What partially fills the gap

Three things, none sufficient.

**Simulator studies.** The 1979 NASA study had what the accident record lacks: identical conditions across crews, and survivors. It is the strongest evidence in this whole subject that coordination varies independently of individual skill, precisely because it is not an accident investigation.

**Documented saves.** A handful of cases where crews handled severe failures and the outcome was survivable — a loss of hydraulic control in 1989, a fuel exhaustion glide in 1983, a dual engine rollback on approach in 2008. Published analysis has credited coordination in some. But the corpus's evidence here rests substantially on a single 1991 conference paper that microcoded communication in two such events, and one paper is a thin foundation for an entire counterfactual arm.

**Confidential reporting systems.** These capture coordination catches by design, de-identified and non-adjudicated, and are the nearest thing to a denominator. They establish that such events occur, and support no claim about frequency or outcome — no specific published analysis is cited here, so the claim stays where the design leaves it.

## Why this is stated rather than solved

An interactive piece can easily present a corpus as though it were a sample. This is not a sample; it is a census of failures. The selection criteria are published for that reason, and the strongest caution to carry through everything above is that a discipline built on studying accidents will tend to find that accidents are what its subject explains.
