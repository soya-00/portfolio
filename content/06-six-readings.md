# Six readings

Six investigations, six authorities, six different things done with the same class of evidence. Each has a full treatment in an optional layer, and each entry below states what that investigation establishes.

**Tenerife, 1977 · Spain.** The Spanish report rests its conclusions on specific procedural violations: taking off without clearance, disregarding an instruction to stand by, not aborting when the other aircraft reported still being on the runway. The English rendering most readers meet foregrounds crew coordination instead. The causal finding is the same and the emphasis differs, which is a translation-and-circulation effect and not a disagreement between investigators.

**Portland, 1978 · United States.** Treated above. The case where a probable cause named the failure of information to travel across a cockpit.

**Kegworth, 1989 · United Kingdom.** The investigating branch found the crew reacted to the initial engine problem prematurely and contrary to their training, and separately that training patterns of the period made no specific provision for coordination between cabin and flight crew. The clearest instance of an authority locating the failure in the design of training and not in the conduct of a crew.

**Guam, 1997 · United States.** Probable cause: an inadequately briefed and executed non-precision approach, and a failure to monitor and cross-check it. Contributing: fatigue, the operator's inadequate training, and the regulator's inhibition of a ground warning system. National culture is not among them.

**Sharm el-Sheikh, 2004 · Egypt.** The Egyptian report stated no probable cause at all, listing four possible causes, and the American and French authorities both dissented publicly, on what the recorded evidence showed. This is the strongest argument in this corpus against treating a recording as self-interpreting, and it is present by citation only, since the issuing authority publishes no reproduction terms.

**Dryden, 1989 · Canada.** A commission of inquiry and not a technical investigation, which placed crew conduct inside an organisational failure reaching scheduling, equipment, management and oversight. The widest frame any authority here applied. Also cite-only.

## Limits of the six

Two of the six cannot be reproduced from, so transcript-level treatment exists for four cases and not the other two, which tracks the licensing policy of the issuing state and not the significance of the investigation. A reader who finds more textual detail on the American and British cases is seeing copyright law, not editorial judgment.
