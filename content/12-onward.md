# Onward

Matched to the sections actually read, with honest access status. Nothing below is recommended without saying what it will cost to reach.

**If the legal comparison was the part that interested you.** The instruments themselves, in their own languages, are freely readable: Indonesia's aviation law via the national legal database, South Korea's investigation act via the national law information centre, France's transport code via Légifrance, Brazil's aeronautical code via the presidential legislative site. All open. The ICAO Annex that sets the international floor is purchase-only, which you should know before you look for it. The Canadian statute is open and its section 28 is the most litigated provision in this area.

**If the origin story was the part that interested you.** The 1979 simulator study and the workshop proceedings that named the discipline are both open, both US government works, both free to read and reproduce. The canonical 1999 generational history is behind a journal paywall, though a copy circulates on a regulator's site whose authorisation status is unclear. The 1990 effectiveness paper is genuinely open.

**If the culture argument was the part that interested you.** The investigating authority's report is open. The general methodological critique of the national-culture framework is in a management journal, paywalled. The aviation replication study is paywalled. The book-length critique of Western aviation concepts in East Asian contexts is purchase-only. The most-read rebuttal is a blog post and free. The asymmetry of that list is the argument in miniature.

**If you want the accident reports themselves.** US and UK reports are open and reproducible, Spain's are reusable under a public-sector information statute, Indonesia's and Taiwan's are open to read, Germany's are readable but restricted for reuse, Egypt's is reachable only through a foreign authority's archive, and Canada's commission volumes are in the government publications catalogue.

## A closing note on datedness

The research cutoff is 16 August 2026. At that date, an appellate conviction in France was under appeal to a higher court, an Indian investigation was projecting a draft report for October 2026, and a Korean investigation had published structural findings but not concluded. All three will move on, while this piece stays where it is.

That is a deliberate limitation and not an oversight. A dated publication that says what it knew and when is more useful than a living document whose earlier claims quietly disappear.
