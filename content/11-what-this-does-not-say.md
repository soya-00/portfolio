# What this does not say

Most publications show you what they found. This one also shows what it looked for and did not find, because in a subject this heavily retold the difference between *established* and *widely repeated* is the main thing a reader needs.

Ten claims were examined and did not survive. Each is listed with what was searched before it was cut.

**Cut outright — the claim does not appear anywhere in this piece:**

- A quotation attributed to a 1969 book on investigations blaming the pilot. Not traceable to a page in either edition. Not converted to a paraphrase either: a paraphrase of a quotation nobody could locate is a fabrication with the evidence removed.
- That a 1989 UK accident prompted the renaming of Cockpit to Crew Resource Management. No source found; chronologically impossible as an origin claim.
- That US manufacturer assistance drove Chinese CRM adoption in the 1990s. Not found in Chinese-language regulatory sources.
- A specific figure for an internal Russian carrier CRM training standard. Sourced only to blogs across three search rounds.

**Rescued — searched again and found:**

- The founding researchers' 1990 statement that the effectiveness question remained open. The journal issue is open-access and the wording is in the freely available text.
- Indonesian regulatory causation, re-sourced from an EU operating ban regulation and a US category downgrade after the original material was found to rest partly on generated text, which is not evidence at any confidence level.
- Union and safety-agency statements previously quoted without attribution.

**Flagged and retained with the limitation visible:**

- That an aviation-specific critique exists of applying national culture scores to individual cockpit conduct. Two rounds, not located. The general critique is real and is cited as general.
- That the insurance industry drove CRM adoption. No evidence found, and that absence is reported in the section above rather than buried here.
- Two phrases quoted from a 2026 appellate judgment. The conviction, the fine and the pending further appeal are all confirmed; one phrase was not located and does not appear.

## Why publish this

Because the alternative is a piece that looks more complete than it is. Every item above is a place where a confident sentence could have been written and would have been wrong, or unverifiable, or true only in the sense that everyone says it.
