# What this is, and what it is not

Between 1972 and 2026, investigators in a dozen countries listened to recordings of people at work in the last minutes before an aircraft was destroyed. They were listening for different things. They were permitted to do different things with what they heard. And in several countries they were forbidden, by statute, from letting anyone else hear it at all.

This is a comparison of those readings. It asks a question that is genuinely open: **what did investigators conclude the recordings meant, and how much did those conclusions actually shape the training discipline that followed?**

The conventional answer is that the recordings revealed a problem — crews failing to coordinate — and that Crew Resource Management was the response. Tidy, widely repeated, not obviously wrong. Also not established, and not assumed here. Regulatory pressure, institutional fashion, union advocacy, manufacturer influence and insurance economics are all candidates, and they are not equally documented. Where the record cannot settle it, this says so.

## Rules this follows

**Nothing appears here that is not in the underlying corpus.** Every factual claim resolves to a source record carrying a verification tier, so a claim resting on a summary is marked differently from one resting on the instrument itself.

**No original analysis of transcripts.** Every statement about what a crew did, said or failed to do is attributed to a named investigating authority or a published study. Where authorities disagree, both readings appear and neither is adjudicated.

**No cockpit audio.** No recordings, reconstructions, waveforms or audio-derived visualisation. Transcripts are documents, reproduced only where the issuing authority's terms permit it.

**Crew appear by role only** — Captain, First Officer, Flight Engineer, Controller — everywhere a reader can see, including page titles, alt text and metadata. Names remain in the cited reports.

**Not a training product, not a memorial, not a database.** Commercial air transport only.

**Not maintained.** Research cutoff 16 August 2026. Links will rot; cases described as unsettled will settle. The design assumes that rather than promising otherwise.

## What the reading path costs

The core path runs about twenty-five minutes and covers the whole territory at survey depth. Everything below it is optional: deeper layers, source apparatus, and the six extended cases. Nothing in an optional layer is required to follow the argument, and no section requires any other section first — a substantial share of readers will arrive mid-piece from a citation or a share, and every section is written to be read cold.

## A note on the square brackets

Where a quotation appears with a role in square brackets — `[the Captain]` — a name has been removed and the role put in its place. Nothing else has been changed. An ellipsis means something was left out; brackets mean something was replaced. The fuller reasoning, and what the practice costs, is set out in the note on redaction.
