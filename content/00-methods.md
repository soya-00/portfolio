# Terms of the comparison

Between 1972 and 2026, investigators in a dozen countries listened to recordings of people at work in the last minutes before an aircraft was destroyed, listening for different things in each case, permitted to do different things with what they heard, and in several countries they were forbidden, by statute, from letting anyone else hear it at all.

This is a comparison of those readings, and it proceeds from a question that remains genuinely open: **what did investigators conclude the recordings meant, and how much did those conclusions actually shape the training discipline that followed?**

The conventional answer holds that the recordings revealed a problem of crews failing to coordinate, and that Crew Resource Management was the response. That account is tidy, widely repeated and not obviously wrong, although it is not established, and it is not assumed here. Regulatory pressure, institutional fashion, union advocacy, manufacturer influence and insurance economics are all candidates, and they are not equally documented. Where the record cannot settle the question, this says so.

## Rules this follows

**Nothing appears here that is not in the underlying corpus.** Every factual claim resolves to a source record carrying a verification tier, so that a claim resting on a summary is marked differently from one resting on the instrument itself.

**No original analysis of transcripts.** Every statement about what a crew did, said or failed to do is attributed to a named investigating authority or a published study. Where authorities disagree, both readings appear and neither is adjudicated.

**No cockpit audio.** No recordings, reconstructions, waveforms or audio-derived visualisation. Transcripts are documents, reproduced only where the issuing authority's terms permit it.

**Crew appear by role only**, as Captain, First Officer, Flight Engineer or Controller, everywhere a reader can see, including page titles, alt text and metadata. Names remain in the cited reports.

**Not a training product, not a memorial, not a database.** Commercial air transport only.

**Not maintained.** Research cutoff 16 August 2026. Links will rot, and cases described here as unsettled will settle. The design assumes as much, and promises nothing else.

## The reading path

The core path runs about twenty-five minutes and covers the whole territory at survey depth. Everything below it is optional: the deeper layers, the source apparatus and the six extended cases. Nothing in an optional layer is required in order to follow the argument, and no section depends on any other section having been read first, since a substantial share of readers will arrive mid-piece from a citation or a share, and every section is written to be read cold.

## Square brackets

Where a quotation appears with a role in square brackets, as in `[the Captain]`, a name has been removed and the role put in its place, and nothing else has been changed. An ellipsis marks an omission, whereas brackets mark a substitution. The fuller reasoning, together with what the practice costs, is set out in the note on redaction.
